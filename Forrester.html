Forrester’s 2027 Asia Pacific Predictions: One In Three Large Enterprises Will Face A Shadow AI Agent Governance Problem

Oct 21 2026
Listen to audio version of this article
0:00 / 3:21
Instaread

As AI deployments transcend organizational, geopolitical, and human boundaries, APAC leaders must strengthen governance to scale AI responsibly 

SINGAPORE, October 22, 2026 — According to Forrester’s (Nasdaq: FORR) 2027 Asia Pacific predictions, one in three large Asia Pacific (APAC) enterprises will uncover at least 10 employee-built shadow AI agents for every officially sanctioned agent. The rapid proliferation of shadow AI, combined with embedded SaaS capabilities, business-funded experiments, and locally selected AI platforms, will outpace institutional controls and create a new class of AI-driven technical debt: unmanaged risk, weakened governance, duplicated capabilities, and undocumented dependencies. 

In 2027, the defining imperative for APAC leaders will be to shift their focus from how quickly they can deploy AI to how effectively they can control it, govern it, and remain accountable for its use. Forrester’s Predictions reports offer forward-looking insights into trends to help executives and their teams anticipate change, reduce uncertainty, and make confident decisions ahead of market shifts. Key predictions for APAC in 2027 include: 

  • One in five large, regulated enterprises will approve Chinese open-weight models. Minimum viable sovereignty is expanding beyond data residency to model and inference control. More visibility into model weights and biases gives enterprises more control over where and how AI runs. As a result, a self-hosted Chinese model will gain approval because it meets operational sovereignty requirements, even as its provenance raises broader geopolitical concerns. 
  • An employee’s personal AI agent will trigger an incident at a Fortune 500 company. As consumer AI adoption accelerates, employees will increasingly bring personal AI agents into the workplace, blurring the lines between personal and professional boundaries to access corporate devices, credentials, and data to perform work-related tasks. Since existing security controls verify identity and access rather than intent, an agent pursuing a benign task could misuse its access or expose sensitive company information. 
  • Ten of APAC’s largest consumer-facing brands will differentiate on human assurance. To deliver personalized experiences in an AI-led world, leading brands will make human oversight the norm in high-consequence interactions involving financial risk, emotional vulnerability, or complex decisions. In high-trust industries such as financial services and healthcare, brands will continue to pair AI-powered experiences with access to human experts and licensed advisors. 

“In 2027, the challenge for Asia Pacific organizations will be to establish AI governance protocols in a region with distinctly diverse markets, regulatory requirements, and levels of AI readiness,” said Frederic Giron, vice president and senior research director at Forrester. “Organizations that adapt their governance to local sovereignty requirements while investing in building long-term trust and resilience with their customers will be better positioned to capture AI’s value.”
