Slayyyter’s ‘crank 2’ is fiercer than the single that broke her biggest album

By Alice Lange
14 September 2026, 10:00 pm · 2 min read

Slayyyter has always treated anger as a production choice, but “crank 2” finds a new gear. The follow-up to her earlier track “Crank” arrives with a sharpened Jersey club pulse and a vocal delivery that critics described as “more sinister” and a “dizzying, ominous threat” — two assessments that say more about her current creative mode than any stream count could.

The track is the second single from “Worst Man in America,” her upcoming fourth studio album, and extends a lyrical thread she started pulling through “Brand New Chanels” — a song about betrayal in the immediate aftermath of her previous record. The connective tissue is explicit: Slayyyter is building a narrative across singles, not simply releasing music into the cycle. Where her previous album captured the sound of someone reclaiming the story, “crank 2” sounds like someone who has internalized the lesson and decided the only direction worth going is further in.

The production, from Austin Corona and Wyatt Bernard, fuses Jersey club (a genre rooted in Newark’s dancefloor scenes, built on a skittering, off-center rhythmic pattern) with the hyperpop sensibility that has defined her catalogue. The combination is deliberately abrasive. Mainstream singles tend to arrive compressed and smoothed for playlist fit; “crank 2” seems designed to resist that pull entirely.

That resistance is also where the track’s limits show. Jersey club works best as a total commitment — a sound that builds across an album or a set, not a format optimized for single discovery. At three minutes and nineteen seconds, “crank 2” arrives, escalates, and ends before the production logic fully unfolds. Whether the sound can carry a full studio record is the structural bet “Worst Man in America” will need to resolve. The single also points squarely at an audience already inside the Slayyyter ecosystem rather than expanding toward a new one — which is either a principled creative decision or a ceiling, depending on the strength of the album behind it.

Slayyyter directed the video herself, a decision that signals how closely she holds the project. Shot in a desert setting, it places her in front of dancers working through a choreography that reviewers called “militant” — structured, synchronized, anchored by an American flag with her own logo where the insignia would normally be. The tornado sirens she heard growing up in St. Louis surface as a sonic reference, grounding a stylized visual in something specifically regional and autobiographical. The effect is a visual identity that reads as hers rather than a label-produced treatment.

The single had its live debut at The Pageant in St. Louis on July 26, 2026, months ahead of its streaming release, to an audience already familiar with the track. “Worst Man in America,” Slayyyter’s fourth studio album, has not yet been given a confirmed release date.
